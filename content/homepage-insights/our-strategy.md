## Start with the question behind the keyword

A keyword is a clue.

![A content strategy moves from expert evidence to audience research, linked articles, discovery, measurement, and revision.](/homepage-pillars/research/connected-content-strategy.png)

*Evidence enters the system, useful answers leave it, and measured results guide the next revision.*

It is not a strategy.

The useful question is: what is the searcher trying to decide?

Someone searching for integration cost may need a budget range. Another person may be comparing an API with a manual workflow. One article will not serve both readers unless it makes those paths clear.

## 1. Set a commercial boundary

Define what the company can prove and deliver.

List the services, products, customer problems, regions, and technical limits that matter. This prevents a common failure: attracting visits for subjects with no link to the business.

Traffic is qualified only when the reader's need overlaps with a real capability.

## 2. Collect questions and evidence

Use sales calls, support tickets, product documents, search data, and specialist interviews. Keep the details specialists omit because they seem obvious.

Each planned page needs:

- one primary question;
- a clear reader and decision;
- first-hand evidence or a named source;
- scope, limits, and trade-offs;
- a relevant next action.

Google's [people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) stresses original analysis, visible expertise, accurate authorship, and a satisfying answer.

## 3. Build connected answers

Group related questions around a central subject. Write one strong overview. Add focused pages for comparisons, costs, implementation choices, risks, and common failures.

Use plain internal links. Google's [link guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable) explains that crawlable links help Google discover pages. Relevant anchor text helps people and Google understand where a link leads.

## 4. Make the answer easy to use

Open with the direct answer. Follow it with evidence, examples, and limits. Use descriptive headings. Place explanatory images beside the text they clarify.

[Google's SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) recommends clear titles, short sections, relevant images, and descriptive alt text. It also states that there is no magic word count.

## 5. Improve from evidence

Review new queries, relevant clicks, movement into commercial pages, qualified enquiries, and the questions readers still ask.

Keep what works. Repair the weak step. Do not publish another article when an existing one needs a better answer.
