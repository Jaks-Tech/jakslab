## Begin with a baseline

Before changing the content, we establish what is already happening. The baseline may include search impressions, ranking queries, organic visits, engaged sessions, conversions, and the pages that assist enquiries.

This prevents teams from judging progress through isolated ranking changes or one unusually successful article.

## Measure visibility and relevance

Early progress often appears as increased impressions across a broader set of relevant searches. We look at whether the company is becoming visible for the subjects, problems, and comparisons that matter to its work.

More traffic is useful only when the new audience is relevant.

## Observe what readers do

Engagement signals help reveal whether an article satisfies the reader’s intent. Useful measures include movement between related articles, visits to service pages, repeat visits, sign-ups, and enquiries that begin from organic content.

No single metric is enough. The pattern across several signals is more informative.

## Connect content to business outcomes

For longer sales cycles, a reader may encounter several articles before contacting the company. We therefore consider assisted conversions and the content mentioned during sales conversations, not only last-click attribution.

Qualitative evidence matters too. Better-informed enquiries, recurring customer questions, and articles reused by sales teams all indicate that the content is doing useful work.

## Improve according to evidence

Measurement should lead to a decision. An article may need a clearer answer, stronger examples, better internal links, a more relevant next step, or a complete repositioning.

The goal is a repeatable improvement cycle: observe, understand, update, and measure again.
