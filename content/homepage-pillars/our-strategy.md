## Start with the business, not a keyword list

The first step is understanding what the company does well, which problems it solves, and which customer questions appear before a purchase or project begins. This creates a useful boundary for the content strategy.

Without that boundary, keyword research can produce topics that attract visitors but have little connection to the company’s capabilities.

## Build a map of real questions

We research the language people use across search engines, AI-assisted discovery, communities, product comparisons, and sales conversations. Questions are grouped by intent:

- learning about a problem;
- comparing possible approaches;
- evaluating a product or provider;
- and preparing to take action.

This map reveals where content is missing, duplicated, outdated, or too vague to be useful.

## Create connected content

Instead of publishing isolated articles, we build connected topic paths. A strong foundational article explains the central subject. Supporting articles answer narrower questions, comparisons, implementation concerns, and common objections.

Internal links then help readers and search systems understand how the ideas relate. Each article has a clear role within the larger structure.

## Optimize what already exists

New writing is only one part of the process. Existing articles may already have authority, backlinks, or useful material. We review them for search intent, technical accuracy, clear structure, internal linking, outdated information, and the next useful action for the reader.

## Measure, learn, and improve

Content optimization is continuous. Search behavior changes, competitors improve, and company offerings evolve. We monitor what earns impressions, attracts the right readers, and contributes to enquiries or product interest. Those findings guide the next round of improvements.
